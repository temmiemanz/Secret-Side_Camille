active Assembly
Build animate humanoid minions from scrap, junk, or dead organic material by hand. Every piece bonds instantly on contact — only the user can remove them. Minions understand user intent perfectly without words or gestures. Not smart, but completely loyal and accurate. Fallen minions can be cannibalized, their parts stripped and reused. No mental toll — building and holding a conversation happen simultaneously.
Valid minion requires
One head and a torso
At least two arms and at least two legs
Every piece must be something the user can lift themselves
Limits
Living tissue does not respond — dead organic material only.
Minions cannot scout or operate independently — they go where the user goes and handle what comes close.
Lost limbs don't regenerate — must be physically reattached.
Leaving a 5ft radius from attached material causes it to lose adhesion.

lore
Camille's mother, Rose, died when he was eight — killed during a trade gone wrong, trying to secure medicine for the hemophilia she and his father Alexios both carried. She managed to take down the person who stabbed her before she bled out. His father heard the commotion. Camille followed. He still remembers the floor.
After that, Alex kept them moving — never staying anywhere long enough to become a target, building a quiet network of people he trusted enough to trade with. Medicine was rationed carefully because there was never enough. Eventually they found a hut in the woods and Alex made it a home, spending years teaching Camille how to take care of himself. It wasn't much, but it was theirs.
When Camille was fourteen, Alex came back with a cut on his arm — nothing serious on its own, but hemophilia and a lack of proper supplies turned it into an infection. Camille went looking for help. He found a traveling medical group led by a man named Erick and came back with them after a week and a half away. He came back too late. Alex died that night. Camille asked Erick if he could join as a student before he finished crying.
The next four years were the closest thing to stability Camille had found. He and another student, Tucker, became inseparable — learning medicine, moving with the group, helping anyone who asked. Then a group asked for help and led them into an ambush. Erick, Tucker, all of them — gone. Camille hid and watched and couldn't do anything. By the time it was over, so was everything he'd rebuilt.
He tried to go back to medical work after that. He couldn't. The sight of blood made him physically sick. Injured people triggered panic he couldn't talk himself out of. What he found he could do was clean things. Organize things. Keep his hands moving and his mind off the parts of the past he couldn't look at. It became a habit. Then a need. The lab coat stays on. Everything has a place. As long as there's something to put in order, he can keep going.
